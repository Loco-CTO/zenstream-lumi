# Common Pile v0.1 source and fit audit

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-03

**Decision:** Keep Common Pile as an English-only source lead for possible later study. Do not admit it to Lumi, use it for tokenizer fitting or training, score it as Lumi evaluation, or treat any public split as a final holdout.

## Finding

The Common Pile is a substantial, carefully curated collection, but it does not close Lumi's bilingual data gap. Its authors describe v0.1 as an 8 TB collection drawn from 30 sources. They state that the collection focuses primarily on English and that the preprocessing used for the Comma training mixture filters non-English text with FastText. The paper and release do not establish a Japanese or English/Japanese code-switch subset or report a Japanese coverage count. This means Comma's filtered mixture is not a supported multilingual base for Lumi; it does not prove that the raw collection contains no Japanese text.

The dataset may later be considered as a source for a small English-only ablation, if that experiment is useful and if the exact documents, rights, workload, and distribution obligations are reviewed first. Its 8 TB aggregate size does not justify ingesting the release wholesale for Lumi. The paper's 7B Comma models trained on 1T and 2T tokens are published results at a different scale, not a compute plan or quality prediction for Lumi.

## Scope and evidence

This is a release-level screen of the authors' Common Pile v0.1 raw collection, its source descriptions, and its stated filtering pipeline. It does not download, inspect, or admit document text. The primary evidence is the authors' NeurIPS 2025 paper and its source appendices, the Common Pile collection maintained by the project, and the source-preparation repository. The collection page identifies v0.1 as an 8 TB release with 30 source items; the paper records the per-source selection methods and the authors' licensing caveats.

The MIT license in the collection-preparation GitHub repository covers that repository's code. It is not a blanket license for the collected text. The paper describes different source and item bases, including public-domain works, CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, Open Parliament License, GFDL, and selected software licenses. The authors explicitly discuss license laundering, noisy license signals, and copyrighted material quoted inside otherwise public-domain/open documents. Their review is useful evidence about their curation; it is not a Lumi legal clearance or a replacement for a source-and-item manifest.

## Source inventory

The groups below account for the 30 source groups shown in the paper. Rights descriptions are the Common Pile authors' reported selection rules, not an independent confirmation of each underlying work.

| Source group | Reported selection and rights basis | Lumi fit and remaining issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific and scholarly: peS2o, PubMed Central, arXiv full papers, arXiv abstracts | peS2o is filtered to English papers with CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, or public-domain declarations; PMC papers are selected from journal license metadata for CC BY, CC BY-SA, or CC0; arXiv full text is limited to CC BY, CC BY-SA, and CC0; arXiv metadata abstracts are treated as CC0. | Mostly formal and technical prose, with per-paper license and quotation lineage to preserve. It is not a conversational media-request source. The paper does not provide a Lumi-ready Japanese/code-switch slice. |
| Online discussions: StackExchange, GitHub Archive, Ubuntu IRC | StackExchange material comes from community/archived dumps under CC BY-SA. GitHub issue, pull request, and comment threads are retained only for repositories whose detected licenses pass the authors' open-license test. Ubuntu-hosted IRC logs are reported as public domain. | These sources offer dialogue-like structure but are largely technical, public, and domain-skewed. Preserve author/repository/source lineage, attribution, and any privacy or identifying-content review. |
| Government and legal: USGPO, USPTO, Caselaw Access Project/Court Listener, UK Hansard, Regulations.gov | The authors select U.S. government and patent works as public domain, include only public-domain legal decisions from CAP/Court Listener, use UK Hansard under the Open Parliament License, and collect federal-agency regulatory documents from Regulations.gov. | Formal legal and procedural language is a poor proxy for everyday requests. Preserve the exact agency/document scope; the Regulations.gov platform also hosts public feedback, so admission must match the authors' stated federal-agency-document subset rather than assume every site submission is cleared. |
| Curated task data: Data Provenance Initiative sources | The authors filter for English or code, non-model-generated text, an open-license audit, and original sources limited to recognized public-domain sources. Component datasets retain their own licenses. | Some task formatting may be relevant, but exact source IDs and licenses vary. It is not a substitute for reviewed ZenStream cases and must not enter the evaluation holdout. |
| Public-domain books: Biodiversity Heritage Library, pre-1929 books, Library of Congress Selected Digitized Books, Project Gutenberg | The reported subsets use source/license metadata, U.S. publication dates, or item-level public-domain metadata; the LoC and Gutenberg subsets described by the paper are English. | Long-form, historical, OCR-derived text offers broad language exposure but weak coverage of modern conversational English and no demonstrated Japanese or code-switch balance. Public-domain claims remain jurisdiction- and item-specific. |
| Open educational resources: DOAB, PressBooks, OERCommons, LibreTexts | The paper selects English resources under public domain, CC BY, or CC BY-SA; LibreTexts also includes GFDL material. DOAB items receive an additional manual check for license statements in book text. | Educational prose is not user-assistant interaction. CC BY-SA/GFDL and attribution obligations require a distribution-specific review; do not infer the eventual weight license from the collection's openness criteria. |
| Wikis: Wikimedia, WikiTeam | The Wikimedia subset uses official March 2025 dumps of English-language wikis. WikiTeam dumps are selected using Internet Archive license metadata for CC BY, CC BY-SA, or public domain, followed by filtering for examples of apparent license laundering. | No Japanese Wikimedia slice is described in the paper's Common Pile pipeline. WikiTeam's reliance on archive metadata leaves residual item-level provenance risk. Share-alike and attribution questions remain open for model artifacts. |
| Source code and specifications: Stack V2, Python Enhancement Proposals | Stack V2 files are selected through the upstream Software Heritage/BigCode license tooling; all detected repository licenses must pass the authors' test. Most PEPs are public domain; five under the Open Publication License are omitted. | Code may help structured-format exposure but is not natural-language target coverage. License diversity is high, and retained code should not be mistaken for natural-language or action-quality evidence. |
| Transcribed speech: Creative Commons YouTube | The authors manually curate more than 2,000 channels described as consistently publishing original CC BY speech videos, then transcribe selected videos with Whisper. | Spoken lectures, tutorials, reviews, essays, speeches, and vlogs are not the same as spontaneous media requests. Retain the exact creator/video license and attribution evidence, and review transcript quality and personal information. No Japanese/code-switch balance is established for Lumi. |
| Web and editorial text: CCCC, Foodista, News, Public Domain Review | CCCC uses CC BY, CC BY-SA, or CC0 signals plus manual checks of high-volume domains; Foodista is reported CC BY; the selected news sites use CC BY or CC BY-SA; Public Domain Review articles use CC BY-SA. | The paper notes that license detection can be wrong and that domain-level review cannot remove every ambiguous case. Exact site, work, author, license revision, and transformation records would be required. The register is not a ZenStream benchmark. |

The Common Pile authors describe licensing as a due-diligence process and explicitly note unresolved risks such as license laundering, third-party quotations, and changed source statements. That limitation matters for Lumi's distribution choice. A public-domain or CC BY item and a CC BY-SA/GFDL item cannot be treated as interchangeable, and the project has not decided whether model weights will be distributed. This audit therefore makes no claim that any subset is approved for a public or commercial Lumi artifact.

## Lumi disposition

| Use | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English-only pretraining comparison | Not approved; retain as a candidate for a later narrow ablation | No exact document manifest, Lumi rights review, bilingual balancing plan, or declared weight-distribution scope exists. |
| Japanese or code-switch pretraining | Not supported by the reviewed evidence | The paper reports primarily English data; its Comma preprocessing filters non-English text; no Japanese or EN/JA inventory was reported. |
| Tokenizer fitting | Not approved | Fitting on this source alone would not represent the target English/Japanese/code-switch distribution, and tokenizer input must pass the same data-use/provenance gate as training input. |
| Development evaluation | Not admitted | Public text can be memorized or encountered during training, and the sources do not provide a reviewed ZenStream media-intent evaluation set. |
| Final holdout | Exclude | The released collection is public and was used in published model training; it cannot serve as a sequestered Lumi holdout. |
| Current action | No download or use | No Common Pile text has been admitted or used by Lumi. |

Before reconsidering any subset, the project must select its weight-distribution target; identify exact source IDs, revisions, and documents; preserve per-item rights, attribution, and processing lineage; review privacy and third-party content; estimate English/Japanese/code-switch token and storage costs; and obtain a use-specific rights review for training and resulting artifacts. Any ShareAlike compatibility question must be resolved explicitly rather than inferred from the phrase “openly licensed.”
