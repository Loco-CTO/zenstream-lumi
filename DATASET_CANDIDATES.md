# Candidate data and benchmark sources

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-02
**Decision:** Research only. No corpus has been downloaded into the project, admitted, or used for training.

This scan reviews English, Japanese, and English/Japanese code-switching source candidates and benchmark leads with traceable origin and usable permissions. A dataset or repository license is not, by itself, proof that every underlying work or human-added annotation is cleared. Lumi will admit data only after the exact source items, rights evidence, transformation history, intended use, and evaluation split are recorded. The current source manifest is collection-level; item-level evidence is required wherever a collection contains works with different rights or origins, and separately authored additions need their own rights evidence.

## Excluded or blocked pending stronger evidence

- **FineWeb2 / Common Crawl text:** FineWeb2 is distributed under ODC-By and documents reproducible processing of Common Crawl. Common Crawl describes its holdings as raw web page data. The collection/database license and crawl availability do not establish the rights for each underlying page. Exclude under the current source-confidence bar unless a future subset has item-level permissions and terms evidence.
- **J-CHAT:** its current dataset card gates access to users agreeing to use it solely for the purpose in Article 30-4 of Japan's Copyright Act and says commercial use is not admitted. That does not meet Lumi's present reuse goals; exclude unless its terms change and are independently reviewed.
- **JESC subtitle corpus:** the original paper says it was assembled by crawling and aligning subtitle text found on the web. The reviewed sources do not establish rights for every underlying subtitle work, so exclude pending item-level rights evidence.

## Admission requirements

Before any candidate moves into `provenance/data_sources.json` as approved:

1. Pin the exact release, revision, source URL, access date, and cryptographic hashes. For mixed collections, record each training unit's original item ID/URL, source revision, rights evidence, and content hash in a document-level ledger; the collection-level manifest alone is insufficient.
2. Record the license or public-domain basis and evidence for each item, every attribution obligation, and whether training, modification, redistribution, and commercial use are permitted. For public-domain claims, record the relevant work, authorship/translation chain, jurisdiction, and basis instead of treating a repository label as proof.
3. Record extraction, normalization, filtering, deduplication, language labels, and split assignment as versioned operations. Preserve a mapping from each training sample back to all source records; record synthetic data's generator, model/version, prompt/template, and human review separately.
4. Check personal or sensitive content and respect source-specific opt-outs or removal requests. Keep source text out of the public code repository when the source terms or privacy review do not permit redistribution; provenance records can use IDs, URLs, hashes, and local controlled storage.
5. Freeze development and final holdout sets before training or synthetic generation. Public benchmark material may be useful for diagnostics, but cannot be represented as a secret or uncontaminated holdout.

These checks are a research gate, not a legal opinion. No source listed here is cleared for Lumi use.
